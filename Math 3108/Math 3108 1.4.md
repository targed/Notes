## Section 1.4: The Matrix Equation Ax = b
### Matrix-Vector Products
  
  If $A$ is an $m \times n$ matrix and $\mathbf{x}$ is a vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$, then the product $A\mathbf{x}$ is the **linear combination of the columns of** $A$ **using the corresponding entries in** $\mathbf{x}$ **as weights.**
  
  **Symbolically:**
  
  If $A = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{a}_1 & \mathbf{a}_2 & ... & \mathbf{a}_n \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ ... \\ x_n \end{bmatrix}$, then:
  
  $A\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{a}_1 & \mathbf{a}_2 & ... & \mathbf{a}_n \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ ... \\ x_n \end{bmatrix} = x_1\mathbf{a}_1 + x_2\mathbf{a}_2 + ... + x_n\mathbf{a}_n$
  
  **Note:** The product $A\mathbf{x}$ yields a vector in $\mathbb{R}^m$.
### Example 1
  
  Compute the product:
  
  $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -3 & 5 & -7 \\ 2 & 4 & -6 & 8 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -3 \\ 1 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}$
### Matrix-Vector Products: The Row-Vector Rule
  
  If the product $A\mathbf{x}$ is defined, then the $i$th entry of the resulting vector is the sum of the products of the corresponding entries from row $i$ of $A$ and the vector $\mathbf{x}$.
### Example 2
  
  Consider the system of linear equations:
  
  $x_1 + 2x_2 = -5$
  $3x_1 + 4x_2 = 1$
  
  Rewrite this system as a vector equation. Then, rewrite the linear combination on the left side as a matrix-vector product.
### The Matrix Equation Ax = b
  
  If $A$ is an $m \times n$ matrix with columns $\mathbf{a}_1, ..., \mathbf{a}_n$, and if $\mathbf{b}$ is in $\mathbb{R}^m$, then the **matrix equation**
  
  $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$
  
  has the same solution set as the vector equation
  
  $x_1\mathbf{a}_1 + x_2\mathbf{a}_2 + ... + x_n\mathbf{a}_n = \mathbf{b}$
  
  and the system of linear equations whose augmented matrix is
  
  $\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{a}_1 & \mathbf{a}_2 & ... & \mathbf{a}_n & \mathbf{b} \end{bmatrix}$.
### When is Ax = b Consistent?
  
  The equation $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ has a solution if and only if $\mathbf{b}$ is a linear combination of the columns of $A$.
  
  **A More Challenging Question:**
  
  Is $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ consistent for *all possible* $\mathbf{b}$?
### Example 3
  
  Let $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 9 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \\ b_3 \end{bmatrix}$. Is the equation $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ consistent for all possible $\mathbf{b}$?
### Example 4
  
  Let $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 10 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \\ b_3 \end{bmatrix}$. Is the equation $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ consistent for all possible $\mathbf{b}$?
  
  $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & b_1 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 & b_2 \\ 7 & 8 & 10 & b_3 \end{bmatrix} \sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & b_1 \\ 0 & -3 & -6 & b_2 - 4b_1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & b_1 - 2b_2 + b_3 \end{bmatrix}$
### Theorem
  
  Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix. Then, the following statements are logically equivalent:
  
  a. For each $\mathbf{b}$ in $\mathbb{R}^m$, the equation $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ has a solution.
  b. Each $\mathbf{b}$ in $\mathbb{R}^m$ is a linear combination of the columns of $A$.
  c. The columns of $A$ span $\mathbb{R}^m$.
  d. $A$ has a pivot position in every row.
### Example 5
  
  Let $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 9 \end{bmatrix}$. Can one column of $A$ be written as a linear combination of the other two?
### Example 6
  
  Determine whether every vector in $\mathbb{R}^4$ can be written as a linear combination of the columns of the matrix:
  
  $B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & -4 \\ 0 & 2 & 6 & 7 \\ 2 & 9 & 5 & -7 \end{bmatrix}$
