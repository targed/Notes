## Section 2.5: Matrix Factorizations
### Matrix Factorizations
  
  A **factorization** of a matrix $A$ is an equation that expresses $A$ as the product of two or more matrices.
  
  This section focuses on the **LU factorization**.
### The LU Factorization
  
  If $A$ is an $m \times n$ matrix that can be row reduced to echelon form **without row interchanges**, then $A$ can be written in the form $A = LU$, where:
  
  *   $L$ is an $m \times m$ lower triangular matrix with 1s on the main diagonal.
  *   $U$ is an $m \times n$ echelon form of $A$.
  
  This is called the **LU factorization** of $A$.
### Using the LU Factorization to Solve Ax = b
  
  The LU factorization can be used to solve $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$.
  
  1.  Rewrite the equation as $L(U\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{b}$.
  2.  Since $U\mathbf{x}$ is a vector, we can let $U\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{y}$.
  3.  Solve the pair of equations:
    *   $L\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{b}$
    *   $U\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{y}$
### Example 1
  
  Solve the equation $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ using the LU factorization given below:
  
  $A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 3 & -5 \\ -4 & -5 & 7 \\ 8 & 6 & -8 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 3 & -5 \\ 0 & -2 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -4 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}$
  
  **Solution:**
  
  1.  **Solve $L\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{b}$:**
  
    $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} y_1 \\ y_2 \\ y_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -4 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}$
  
    This corresponds to the system:
    $y_1 = 2$
    $-y_1 + y_2 = -4$
    $2y_1 + y_3 = 6$
  
    Solving by forward substitution:
    $y_1 = 2$
    $y_2 = -4 + y_1 = -4 + 2 = -2$
    $y_3 = 6 - 2y_1 = 6 - 2(2) = 2$
  
    So, $\mathbf{y} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -2 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$
  
  2.  **Solve $U\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{y}$:**
  
    $\begin{bmatrix} 4 & 3 & -5 \\ 0 & -2 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -2 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$
  
    This corresponds to the system:
    $4x_1 + 3x_2 - 5x_3 = 2$
    $-2x_2 + 2x_3 = -2$
    $2x_3 = 2$
  
    Solving by back substitution:
    $x_3 = 1$
    $-2x_2 = -2 - 2x_3 = -2 - 2 = -4 \Rightarrow x_2 = 2$
    $4x_1 = 2 - 3x_2 + 5x_3 = 2 - 3(2) + 5(1) = 1 \Rightarrow x_1 = \frac{1}{4}$
  
    So, $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 1/4 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$
### Finding an LU Factorization
  
  1.  Reduce $A$ to an echelon form $U$ by a sequence of row replacement operations, if possible. (We will not consider the case where this is not possible.)
  2.  Place entries in $L$ such that the same sequence of row replacement operations reduces $L$ to $I$.
### Example 2
  
  Find an LU factorization of $A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -7 & -2 \\ -3 & 5 & 1 \\ 6 & -4 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
  
  **Solution:**
  
  1. **Reduce A to echelon form (U):**
    * $R_2 \rightarrow R_2 + R_1$
    * $R_3 \rightarrow R_3 - 2R_1$
  
    $\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -7 & -2 \\ -3 & 5 & 1 \\ 6 & -4 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \sim \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -7 & -2 \\ 0 & -2 & -1 \\ 0 & 10 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$
    * $R_3 \rightarrow R_3 + 5R_2$
  
      $\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -7 & -2 \\ 0 & -2 & -1 \\ 0 & 10 & 4 \end{bmatrix} \sim \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -7 & -2 \\ 0 & -2 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = U$
  2. **Construct L:**
   Start with I, apply inverse of row operations in reverse order, and stop at I.
   $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$
    * $R_3 \rightarrow R_3 - 5R_2$:
    $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -5 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$
   * $R_3 \rightarrow R_3 + 2R_1$:
    $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & -5 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$
  * $R_2 \rightarrow R_2 - R_1$
  
    $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & -5 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = L$
    Alternatively, you can build `L` as you go along, by taking the coefficients used in the row reduction and placing them, with changed signs, in the spot that turns into a 0.
  
  3.  **Result:** $A = LU = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & -5 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -7 & -2 \\ 0 & -2 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$
### Example 3
  
  Find an LU factorization of $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -4 & -2 & 3 \\ 6 & -9 & -5 & 8 \\ 2 & -7 & -3 & 9 \\ 4 & -2 & -2 & -1 \\ -6 & 3 & 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$.
  
  **Solution:**
  
  1.  **Reduce A to echelon form (U):**
  
    *   $R_2 \rightarrow R_2 - 3R_1$
    *   $R_3 \rightarrow R_3 - R_1$
    *   $R_4 \rightarrow R_4 - 2R_1$
    *   $R_5 \rightarrow R_5 + 3R_1$
  
    $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -4 & -2 & 3 \\ 6 & -9 & -5 & 8 \\ 2 & -7 & -3 & 9 \\ 4 & -2 & -2 & -1 \\ -6 & 3 & 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix} \sim \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -4 & -2 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & -3 & -1 & 6 \\ 0 & 6 & 2 & -7 \\ 0 & -9 & -3 & 13 \end{bmatrix}$
  
    *   $R_3 \rightarrow R_3 + R_2$
    *   $R_4 \rightarrow R_4 - 2R_2$
    *   $R_5 \rightarrow R_5 + 3R_2$
  
    $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -4 & -2 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & -3 & -1 & 6 \\ 0 & 6 & 2 & -7 \\ 0 & -9 & -3 & 13 \end{bmatrix} \sim \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -4 & -2 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & -5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 10 \end{bmatrix}$
  
    *   $R_4 \rightarrow R_4 + R_3$
    *   $R_5 \rightarrow R_5 - 2R_3$
  
    $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -4 & -2 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & -5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 10 \end{bmatrix} \sim \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -4 & -2 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = U$
  
  2.  **Construct L:**
    Build the matrix L.
    $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 3 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 & 1 & 0 & 0\\ 2 & 2 & -1 & 1 & 0 \\ -3 & -3 & 2 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} = L$
  
  3. **Result:**
  $A = LU = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 3 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 & 1 & 0 & 0\\ 2 & 2 & -1 & 1 & 0 \\ -3 & -3 & 2 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -4 & -2 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$
### Numerical Notes
  
  For an $n \times n$ dense matrix $A$ with $n \geq 30$:
  
  *   Computing an LU factorization of $A$ takes about $2n^3/3$ flops, about the same as row reducing $\begin{bmatrix} A & \mathbf{b} \end{bmatrix}$.
  *   Finding $A^{-1}$ requires about $2n^3$ flops.
  *   Solving $L\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{b}$ and $U\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{y}$ requires about $2n^2$ flops.
  *   Multiplication of $\mathbf{b}$ by $A^{-1}$ also requires about $2n^2$ flops, but may result in a less accurate answer than that obtained by LU due to roundoff errors.
  *   If $A$ is sparse, then $L$ and $U$ may also be sparse, whereas $A^{-1}$ is likely to be dense. In this case, we are much better off using $LU$ than $A^{-1}$.
