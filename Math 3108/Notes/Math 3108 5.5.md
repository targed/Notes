## Section 5.5: Complex Eigenvalues
### The Complex Conjugate
  
  The **complex conjugate** of a complex number $z = a + bi$ is $\overline{z} = a - bi$.
### Example 1
  
  Find the conjugates and the real and imaginary parts of each of the following.
  
  *   $z = 5 + 3i$
  *   $z = 4 - 7i$
  *   $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 5-i \\ 2 \\ 3+5i \end{bmatrix}$
  
  **Solution:**
  
  *   $z = 5 + 3i$
    *   Conjugate: $\overline{z} = 5 - 3i$
    *   Real part: $\text{Re}(z) = 5$
    *   Imaginary part: $\text{Im}(z) = 3$
  *   $z = 4 - 7i$
    *   Conjugate: $\overline{z} = 4 + 7i$
    *   Real part: $\text{Re}(z) = 4$
    *   Imaginary part: $\text{Im}(z) = -7$
  *   $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 5-i \\ 2 \\ 3+5i \end{bmatrix}$
    *   Conjugate: $\overline{\mathbf{x}} = \begin{bmatrix} \overline{5-i} \\ \overline{2} \\ \overline{3+5i} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5+i \\ 2 \\ 3-5i \end{bmatrix}$
    *   Real part: $\text{Re}(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{bmatrix} \text{Re}(5-i) \\ \text{Re}(2) \\ \text{Re}(3+5i) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$
    *   Imaginary part: $\text{Im}(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{bmatrix} \text{Im}(5-i) \\ \text{Im}(2) \\ \text{Im}(3+5i) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix}$
### Example 2
  
  Find the eigenvalues and a basis for each eigenspace in $\mathbb{C}^2$.
  
  $A = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & -5 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$
  
  **Solution:**
  
  1.  **Find eigenvalues:** Solve $\text{det}(A - \lambda I) = 0$.
    $A - \lambda I = \begin{bmatrix} 5-\lambda & -5 \\ 1 & 1-\lambda \end{bmatrix}$
    $\text{det}(A - \lambda I) = (5-\lambda)(1-\lambda) - (-5)(1) = 5 - 6\lambda + \lambda^2 + 5 = \lambda^2 - 6\lambda + 10 = 0$.
    Using the quadratic formula:
    $\lambda = \frac{-(-6) \pm \sqrt{(-6)^2 - 4(1)(10)}}{2(1)} = \frac{6 \pm \sqrt{36 - 40}}{2} = \frac{6 \pm \sqrt{-4}}{2} = \frac{6 \pm 2i}{2} = 3 \pm i$.
    The eigenvalues are $\lambda_1 = 3+i$ and $\lambda_2 = 3-i$.
  
  2.  **Find basis for eigenspace for $\lambda_1 = 3+i$:** Solve $(A - (3+i)I)\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$.
    $A - (3+i)I = \begin{bmatrix} 5-(3+i) & -5 \\ 1 & 1-(3+i) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2-i & -5 \\ 1 & -2-i \end{bmatrix}$.
    Row reduce the augmented matrix $\begin{bmatrix} 2-i & -5 & 0 \\ 1 & -2-i & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
    Swap rows: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -2-i & 0 \\ 2-i & -5 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
    $R_2 \rightarrow R_2 - (2-i)R_1$:
    The second row becomes: $-5 - (2-i)(-2-i) = -5 - (-(4+2i-2i-i^2)) = -5 - (-(4-(-1))) = -5 - (-5) = 0$.
    The reduced matrix is $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -2-i & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
    The equation is $x_1 + (-2-i)x_2 = 0$, so $x_1 = (2+i)x_2$. $x_2$ is free.
    $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} (2+i)x_2 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = x_2 \begin{bmatrix} 2+i \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.
    A basis for the eigenspace for $\lambda_1 = 3+i$ is $\left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 2+i \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \right\}$.
  
  3.  **Find basis for eigenspace for $\lambda_2 = 3-i$:** Since the matrix A has real entries, the eigenvalues are complex conjugates, and the corresponding eigenvectors are also complex conjugates.
    The basis for the eigenspace for $\lambda_2 = 3-i$ is the conjugate of the basis for $\lambda_1=3+i$:
    $\left\{ \overline{\begin{bmatrix} 2+i \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}} \right\} = \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 2-i \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \right\}$.
### Complex Eigenvalues
  
  Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix whose entries are real, and let $\lambda$ be an eigenvalue of $A$ with corresponding eigenvector $\mathbf{v}$. Then, $\overline{\lambda}$ is also an eigenvalue of $A$ with corresponding eigenvector $\overline{\mathbf{v}}$.
### Example 3
  
  Show that if $a$ and $b$ are real and not both zero, then the eigenvalues of $C = \begin{bmatrix} a & -b \\ b & a \end{bmatrix}$ are $\lambda = a \pm bi$ with corresponding eigenvectors $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \mp i \end{bmatrix}$.
  
  **Solution:**
  
  1.  **Find eigenvalues:** Solve $\text{det}(C - \lambda I) = 0$.
    $C - \lambda I = \begin{bmatrix} a-\lambda & -b \\ b & a-\lambda \end{bmatrix}$
    $\text{det}(C - \lambda I) = (a-\lambda)(a-\lambda) - (-b)(b) = (a-\lambda)^2 + b^2 = 0$.
    $(a-\lambda)^2 = -b^2$
    $a-\lambda = \pm \sqrt{-b^2} = \pm i\sqrt{b^2} = \pm ib$ (since $b$ is real, $\sqrt{b^2}=|b|$, but $\pm$ covers both cases).
    $\lambda = a \mp ib$. So the eigenvalues are $\lambda = a+ib$ and $\lambda = a-ib$.
  
  2.  **Find eigenvector for $\lambda = a+ib$:** Solve $(C - (a+ib)I)\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$.
    $C - (a+ib)I = \begin{bmatrix} a-(a+ib) & -b \\ b & a-(a+ib) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -ib & -b \\ b & -ib \end{bmatrix}$.
    Augmented matrix: $\begin{bmatrix} -ib & -b & 0 \\ b & -ib & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
    Multiply $R_1$ by $i$: $\begin{bmatrix} (-ib)(i) & (-b)(i) & 0 \\ b & -ib & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} b & -ib & 0 \\ b & -ib & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
    Row reduce: $\begin{bmatrix} b & -ib & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
    If $b \neq 0$, the equation is $bx_1 - ibx_2 = 0 \implies x_1 - ix_2 = 0 \implies x_1 = ix_2$. $x_2$ is free.
    $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} ix_2 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = x_2 \begin{bmatrix} i \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$. Choose $x_2 = i$, eigenvector is $\begin{bmatrix} i^2 \\ i \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ i \end{bmatrix}$. Or choose $x_2 = -i$, eigenvector is $\begin{bmatrix} -i^2 \\ -i \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -i \end{bmatrix}$.
    If $b=0$, then $a\neq 0$. The matrix is $C=\begin{bmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & a \end{bmatrix}$, eigenvalue is $a$. $C-aI = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$. Any nonzero vector is an eigenvector. The statement requires $a,b$ not both zero. If $b \neq 0$, we found $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -i \end{bmatrix}$ as an eigenvector for $a+ib$.
  
  3.  **Find eigenvector for $\lambda = a-ib$:** Since the matrix is real, the eigenvector is the conjugate of the eigenvector for $a+ib$.
    $\overline{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -i \end{bmatrix}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ i \end{bmatrix}$.
    So, for $\lambda = a+ib$, an eigenvector is $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -i \end{bmatrix}$, and for $\lambda = a-ib$, an eigenvector is $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ i \end{bmatrix}$. This matches the prompt if we associate $\lambda = a \pm bi$ with eigenvectors $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \mp i \end{bmatrix}$.
### Example 4
  
  List the eigenvalues of $A = \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{3} & -1 \\ 1 & \sqrt{3} \end{bmatrix}$.
  
  **Solution:**
  This matrix is of the form $C = \begin{bmatrix} a & -b \\ b & a \end{bmatrix}$ with $a = \sqrt{3}$ and $b = 1$.
  From Example 3, the eigenvalues are $\lambda = a \pm bi = \sqrt{3} \pm i(1)$.
  The eigenvalues are $\sqrt{3}+i$ and $\sqrt{3}-i$.
  
  **Recall: Common Transformations**
  
  The standard matrix $A$ of the transformation $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^2$ which rotates each point in $\mathbb{R}^2$ about the origin through an angle $\phi$ (where positive angles correspond to counterclockwise rotation) is
  
  $A = \begin{bmatrix} \cos \phi & -\sin \phi \\ \sin \phi & \cos \phi \end{bmatrix}$
### Example 4 (continued)
  
  The transformation $\mathbf{x} \mapsto A\mathbf{x}$ is the composition of a rotation and a scaling. Give the angle $\phi$ (where $-\pi < \phi \leq \pi$) and give the scale factor $r$.
  
  $A = \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{3} & -1 \\ 1 & \sqrt{3} \end{bmatrix}$
  
  **Solution:**
  
  From the fact below the example, we identify $a = \sqrt{3}$ and $b = 1$.
  The scaling factor is $r = |\lambda| = \sqrt{a^2+b^2} = \sqrt{(\sqrt{3})^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{3+1} = \sqrt{4} = 2$.
  We can write $A = r \begin{bmatrix} a/r & -b/r \\ b/r & a/r \end{bmatrix} = 2 \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{3}/2 & -1/2 \\ 1/2 & \sqrt{3}/2 \end{bmatrix}$.
  This matrix matches the rotation matrix form $\begin{bmatrix} \cos \phi & -\sin \phi \\ \sin \phi & \cos \phi \end{bmatrix}$ where $\cos \phi = \sqrt{3}/2$ and $\sin \phi = 1/2$.
  The angle $\phi$ in the interval $(-\pi, \pi]$ for which this is true is $\phi = \pi/6$.
  Scale factor $r=2$, Rotation angle $\phi = \pi/6$.
### Fact
  
  If $C = \begin{bmatrix} a & -b \\ b & a \end{bmatrix}$ where $a$ and $b$ are real and not both zero, then the eigenvalues of $C$ are $\lambda = a \pm bi$ with eigenvectors $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \mp i \end{bmatrix}$.
  
  Also, if $r = |\lambda| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$, then $C = r \begin{bmatrix} a/r & -b/r \\ b/r & a/r \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} r & 0 \\ 0 & r \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \cos \phi & -\sin \phi \\ \sin \phi & \cos \phi \end{bmatrix}$ so the transformation $\mathbf{x} \mapsto C\mathbf{x}$ is the composition of a rotation through $\phi$ and a scaling by $|\lambda|$.
### Theorem
  
  Let $A$ be a real $2 \times 2$ matrix with a complex eigenvalue $\lambda = a - bi$ (with $b \neq 0$) and an associated eigenvector $\mathbf{v}$ in $\mathbb{C}^2$. Then,
  
  $A = PCP^{-1}$
  
  where $P = \begin{bmatrix} \text{Re } \mathbf{v} & \text{Im } \mathbf{v} \end{bmatrix}$ and $C = \begin{bmatrix} a & -b \\ b & a \end{bmatrix}$.
### Example 5
  
  Find an invertible matrix $P$ and a matrix $C$ of the form $\begin{bmatrix} a & -b \\ b & a \end{bmatrix}$ such that the given matrix has the form $A = PCP^{-1}$.
  
  $A = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & -2 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$
  
  **Solution:**
  
  1.  **Find eigenvalues:** Solve $\text{det}(A - \lambda I) = 0$.
    $A - \lambda I = \begin{bmatrix} 5-\lambda & -2 \\ 1 & 3-\lambda \end{bmatrix}$
    $\text{det}(A - \lambda I) = (5-\lambda)(3-\lambda) - (-2)(1) = 15 - 8\lambda + \lambda^2 + 2 = \lambda^2 - 8\lambda + 17 = 0$.
    Using the quadratic formula:
    $\lambda = \frac{-(-8) \pm \sqrt{(-8)^2 - 4(1)(17)}}{2(1)} = \frac{8 \pm \sqrt{64 - 68}}{2} = \frac{8 \pm \sqrt{-4}}{2} = \frac{8 \pm 2i}{2} = 4 \pm i$.
    Let's choose the eigenvalue $\lambda = a - bi = 4 - i$. So $a=4$ and $b=1$.
  
  2.  **Find the corresponding eigenvector $\mathbf{v}$ for $\lambda = 4-i$:** Solve $(A - (4-i)I)\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$.
    $A - (4-i)I = \begin{bmatrix} 5-(4-i) & -2 \\ 1 & 3-(4-i) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1+i & -2 \\ 1 & -1+i \end{bmatrix}$.
    Row reduce $\begin{bmatrix} 1+i & -2 & 0 \\ 1 & -1+i & 0 \end{bmatrix} \sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1+i & 0 \\ 1+i & -2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
    $R_2 \rightarrow R_2 - (1+i)R_1$:
    $-2 - (1+i)(-1+i) = -2 - (-(1-i+i-i^2)) = -2 - (-(1+1)) = -2 - (-2) = 0$.
    The reduced matrix is $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1+i & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
    $x_1 + (-1+i)x_2 = 0 \implies x_1 = (1-i)x_2$. $x_2$ is free.
    $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} (1-i)x_2 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix}$. Choose $x_2=1$, so $\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 1-i \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.
  
  3.  **Construct P and C:**
    $\text{Re } \mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$
    $\text{Im } \mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$
    $P = \begin{bmatrix} \text{Re } \mathbf{v} & \text{Im } \mathbf{v} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$
    $C = \begin{bmatrix} a & -b \\ b & a \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & -1 \\ 1 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$
  
  4.  **Result:** The matrices are $P = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ and $C = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & -1 \\ 1 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$.
