## Section 1.8: Linear Transformations
### Transformations
  
  A **transformation** (or **function**) $T$ from $\mathbb{R}^n$ to $\mathbb{R}^m$ is a rule that assigns to each vector $\mathbf{x}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ a corresponding vector $T(\mathbf{x})$ in $\mathbb{R}^m$.
  
  *   **Domain:** $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the **domain** of $T$.
  *   **Codomain:** $\mathbb{R}^m$ is the **codomain** of $T$.
  *   **Image:** $T(\mathbf{x})$ is called the **image** of $\mathbf{x}$ under the action of $T$.
  *   **Range:** The **range** of $T$ is the set of all possible images $T(\mathbf{x})$.
### Example 1
  
  Identify the domain, codomain, and range of the transformation $f(x) = x^2$.
### Matrix Transformations
  
  A **matrix transformation** is a transformation defined as $T(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x}$, where $A$ is an $m \times n$ matrix.
  
  *   **Domain:** $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the domain of $T$.
  *   **Codomain:** $\mathbb{R}^m$ is the codomain of $T$.
  *   **Range:** The range of $T$ is the set of all linear combinations of the columns of $A$ (i.e., the column space of $A$).
### Example 2
  
  Let $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ and define $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^2$ by $T(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x}$.
  
  Find the images under $T$ of $\mathbf{u} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \end{bmatrix}$.
### Example 3
  
  Find a vector $\mathbf{x}$ whose image under $T(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x}$ is $\mathbf{b}$, and determine whether $\mathbf{x}$ is unique.
  
  $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -3 & 2 \\ 3 & -8 & 8 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 6 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$
### Linear Transformations
  
  A transformation $T$ is **linear** if:
  
  i. $T(\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = T(\mathbf{u}) + T(\mathbf{v})$ for all $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}$ in the domain of $T$.
  ii. $T(c\mathbf{u}) = cT(\mathbf{u})$ for all scalars $c$ and all $\mathbf{u}$ in the domain of $T$.
  
  **Note:** Every matrix transformation is a linear transformation, but not all linear transformations are matrix transformations.
### We're Doing The Same Thing Again!
  
  **Old:** Solve $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$.
  
  **New:** Find all $\mathbf{x}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ that are mapped into the zero vector by the transformation $\mathbf{x} \mapsto A\mathbf{x}$.
  
  **Old:** Solve $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$.
  
  **New:** Is $\mathbf{b}$ in the range of the linear transformation $\mathbf{x} \mapsto A\mathbf{x}$?
### Example 4
  
  Plot $\mathbf{u} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}$ and their images under the transformation $T(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x}$ for $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$. Describe the transformation geometrically.
  
  **Note:** $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ is called the $2 \times 2$ identity matrix, $I_2$.

### Properties of Linear Transformations
  
  If $T$ is a linear transformation, then:
  
  *   $T(\mathbf{0}) = \mathbf{0}$
  *   $T(c\mathbf{u} + d\mathbf{v}) = cT(\mathbf{u}) + dT(\mathbf{v})$ for all vectors $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}$ in the domain of $T$ and all scalars $c, d$.
### Example 7
  
  Let $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^2$ be a linear transformation that maps $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$ to $\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ to $\begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix}$. Find the images of $\begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix}$.
