### **1.7 A Brief History of the Internet**
  
  The history of computer networking and the internet can be broken down into five distinct eras, showing an evolution from theoretical principles to the massive, commercial, and mobile network we use today.
#### **1. 1961–1972: Early Packet-Switching Principles**
  
  This period was defined by the foundational research that proved packet switching was a viable alternative to the circuit-switched telephone network, especially for the "bursty" traffic generated by computers.
  
  *   **1961:** Leonard Kleinrock's work on **queueing theory** mathematically demonstrated the effectiveness of packet switching.
  *   **1964:** Paul Baran began investigating packet switching for creating robust, survivable military communication networks.
  *   **1967:** The US **Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA)** conceived of the **ARPAnet**, a plan to build the world's first packet-switched computer network.
  *   **1969:** The first ARPAnet node became operational.
  *   **1972:** A landmark year featuring:
    *   The first public demonstration of the ARPAnet.
    *   The completion of the **Network Control Protocol (NCP)**, the first host-to-host protocol and direct ancestor of TCP/IP.
    *   The creation of the first email program by Ray Tomlinson.
    *   By this time, ARPAnet had grown to 15 nodes.
#### **2. 1972–1980: Internetworking and New Networks**
  
  With the success of ARPAnet, the focus shifted to connecting disparate networks together, a concept known as **internetworking**.
  
  *   **1970:** The **ALOHAnet**, a microwave network in Hawaii, became operational.
  *   **1974:** Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn published a seminal paper outlining the principles for interconnecting networks. These principles define the core architecture of today's internet:
    *   **Minimalism and Autonomy:** Individual networks could be interconnected without requiring any internal changes.
    *   **Best-Effort Service:** The network makes no guarantees about packet delivery. Reliability is left to the end systems.
    *   **Stateless Routing:** Routers do not hold state about end-to-end connections.
    *   **Decentralized Control:** No single entity controls the entire network.
  *   **1976:** **Ethernet**, the foundational link-layer technology for wired networks, was invented by Bob Metcalfe at Xerox PARC.
  *   **1979:** ARPAnet grew to 200 nodes.
#### **3. 1980–1990: New Protocols and the Proliferation of Networks**
  
  This decade saw the standardization of the key protocols that still power the internet and a rapid expansion in the number of interconnected networks.
  
  *   **1983:** The official deployment of **TCP/IP** as the new standard protocol suite for ARPAnet, replacing NCP.
  *   **1982:** The **SMTP** protocol for email was defined.
  *   **1983:** The **Domain Name System (DNS)** was defined to translate human-readable names (like www.google.com) into numerical IP addresses.
  *   **1985:** The **FTP** protocol for file transfer was defined.
  *   **1988:** **TCP congestion control** was implemented, a critical mechanism that allows hosts to throttle their sending rate to prevent network collapse.
  *   New national networks emerged, such as **CSnet**, **BITnet**, and **NSFnet**, creating a growing confederation of networks. By the end of the decade, this "network of networks" connected approximately **100,000 hosts**.
#### **4. 1990s & 2000s: Commercialization, the Web, and New Applications**
  
  This era was marked by the transition of the internet from a government/research project to a global commercial platform, driven by the invention of the World Wide Web.
  
  *   **Early 1990s:** The original **ARPAnet was decommissioned**, its role having been superseded by newer networks.
  *   **1991:** The **National Science Foundation (NSF)** lifted restrictions on the commercial use of its NSFnet backbone, officially opening the internet for business. NSFnet itself was later decommissioned in 1995 as private ISPs took over.
  *   **The Birth of the Web:**
    *   Invented by **Tim Berners-Lee** at CERN in the early 1990s.
    *   He developed the key components: **HTML** (the language for creating web pages), **HTTP** (the application-layer protocol), the first web server, and the first web browser.
    *   **1994:** The **Mosaic** browser (which later became Netscape) was released, providing a user-friendly graphical interface that made the web accessible to the general public and ignited its explosive growth.
  *   **Late 1990s – 2000s:**
    *   "Killer apps" like instant messaging and peer-to-peer (P2P) file sharing emerged.
    *   The number of hosts grew from 100,000 to over **50 million**, with more than 100 million users.
    *   Backbone link speeds increased from Mbps to **Gbps**.
#### **5. 2005–Present: Scale, SDN, Mobility, and the Cloud**
  
  The modern era is defined by massive scale, the programmability of networks, and the shift from fixed to mobile access.
  
  *   Aggressive deployment of **broadband home access** (10s to 100s of Mbps).
  *   **2008:** The emergence of **Software-Defined Networking (SDN)**, an approach to making networks more programmable and centrally managed.
  *   Increasing ubiquity of high-speed wireless access, including **4G/5G cellular** and **Wi-Fi**.
  *   Major service providers like **Google, Facebook, and Microsoft** began creating their own massive private global networks to bypass the commercial internet and connect their data centers directly to end users.
  *   Enterprises increasingly run their services in **"the cloud"** on platforms like Amazon Web Services (AWS) and Microsoft Azure.
  *   The rise of **smartphones** led to a major shift towards mobility. Since **2017**, there have been more mobile devices than fixed devices connected to the internet.
