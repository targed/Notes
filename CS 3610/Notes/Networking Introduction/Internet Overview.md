### What is the Internet? 
  
  The internet is a global system of interconnected computer networks that use the TCP/IP protocol suite to communicate between networks and devices. It is a "network of networks," connecting private, public, academic, business, and government networks of local to global scope. This vast network is linked by a broad array of electronic, wireless, and optical networking technologies. 
  
  There are two main ways to view the Internet: 
  
  *   **A "nuts and bolts" view:** This perspective focuses on the tangible components of the internet. This includes billions of connected computing devices (hosts or end systems) that run network applications. Packet switches, like routers and switches, forward packets of data to their destinations. Communication links, including fiber optic cables, copper wires, and radio waves, connect these devices, and networks are collections of these devices and links managed by organizations. 
  *   **A "services" view:** This perspective focuses on the services that the internet provides to applications. The internet's infrastructure supports a wide range of applications, such as the World Wide Web, streaming video, email, and online gaming. It also provides a programming interface that allows developers to create new distributed applications.
### Internet Structure 
  
  The structure of the internet is a complex and ever-evolving "network of networks." At its core, the internet is composed of a series of interconnected Internet Service Providers (ISPs). 
  
  *   **ISPs and Connectivity:** Hosts connect to the internet through ISPs. These ISPs must then interconnect to allow any two hosts to communicate with each other. This creates a complex network of networks that has been shaped by economic and national policies. 
  *   **A Hierarchical Structure:** The internet has a loosely hierarchical structure. At the center are a small number of large, well-connected Tier-1 commercial ISPs with national and international coverage. These Tier-1 ISPs connect to regional ISPs, which in turn connect to local access ISPs that individuals and businesses use to connect to the internet. 
  *   **Content Provider Networks:** In addition to the ISP hierarchy, large content providers like Google and Facebook have built their own private networks. These networks connect their data centers to the internet, often bypassing Tier-1 and regional ISPs to get their content closer to end-users. 
  
  This intricate structure of interconnected networks, from global ISPs to content provider networks, is what enables the seamless flow of information across the globe.
