### **What is the Internet?**
  
  The Internet can be understood from two primary perspectives: a "nuts and bolts" view and a "services" view.
#### **A "Nuts and Bolts" View**
  
  From a hardware and infrastructure standpoint, the Internet is composed of several key components:
  
  *   **Connected Computing Devices:** There are billions of devices connected to the Internet, which are referred to as **hosts** or **end systems**. These devices run network applications and are situated at the "edge" of the Internet.
  *   **Packet Switches:** These devices are responsible for forwarding packets, which are small chunks of data, through the network. The two main types of packet switches are **routers** and **switches**.
  *   **Communication Links:** These are the physical media that connect the various devices on the Internet. They can be made of various materials, including:
    *   Fiber optic cables
    *   Copper wires
    *   Radio waves (wireless)
    *   Satellite links
    The rate at which data can be transmitted over these links is known as the **transmission rate** or **bandwidth**.
  *   **Networks:** A network is a collection of devices, routers, and links that are managed by a single organization. The Internet itself is a "network of networks," consisting of numerous interconnected **Internet Service Providers (ISPs)**.
#### **A "Services" View**
  
  From a user and application perspective, the Internet is an infrastructure that provides services for a wide range of applications. This includes:
  
  *   Web browsing
  *   Video and audio streaming
  *   Multimedia teleconferencing
  *   Email
  *   Online gaming
  *   E-commerce
  *   Social media
  *   Interconnected smart appliances
  
  The Internet also offers a **programming interface** that allows distributed applications to send and receive data. This can be thought of as providing "hooks" for applications to connect to the Internet's transport services, similar to how a postal service provides options for sending and receiving mail.
### **What is a Protocol?**
  
  Protocols are fundamental to the operation of the Internet, governing how messages are sent and received between devices.
  
  *   **Analogy to Human Protocols:** Network protocols are similar to human social protocols. For example, when one person wants to ask another for the time, there's an established sequence of communication (e.g., getting their attention, asking the question, receiving the answer).
  *   **Formal Definition:** A protocol defines the **format** and **order** of messages that are sent and received between network entities, as well as the **actions** that should be taken upon the transmission or receipt of a message.
  *   **Examples:** Common Internet protocols include HTTP (for the web), TCP, IP, and Wi-Fi.
#### **Internet Standards**
  
  The standards for Internet protocols are developed and maintained by specific organizations:
  
  *   **Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF):** This is the primary body responsible for developing and promoting Internet standards.
  *   **Request for Comments (RFC):** The technical and organizational documents from the IETF are published as RFCs.
