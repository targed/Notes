  * **Fundamental science:**  Physics explores the most basic principles governing the universe.
  * **Focus:** Understanding the behavior and structure of matter, from the smallest subatomic particles to the largest galaxies.

  * **It's FUN!:** Physics can be engaging and intellectually stimulating through demonstrations and experiments.

## Kinematics: Describing Motion
  
  * **Point mass:**  Simplifying objects as points to focus on translational motion.
  * **Key aspects of motion:**
    * **Position:**  Where an object is located.
    * **Velocity:** How fast and in what direction an object is moving.
    * **Acceleration:**  How speed and direction of motion change.
## Position
  
  * **Coordinate system:**  A frame of reference for specifying location.
  * **Numerical value ($x$):**  Represents the position along a coordinate axis.
  * **$x(t)$:**  Position as a function of time.
  * **Initial position ($x_0 = x(t_0)$):**  Position at a specific starting time ($t_0$). Note: This doesn't necessarily mean $x_0 = 0$.
## Showing Position
  
  * **x-axis:**  A line with an origin (0) and a positive direction (indicated by an arrow).
  * **Marking position:**  Points on the x-axis indicate an object's location at different times.
## Position versus Time Graphs
  
  * **x-axis:**  Represents time ($t$).
  * **y-axis:** Represents position ($x$).
  * **Points on the graph:** Indicate the object's position at specific times.
  * **Connecting the points:** A curve or line shows how the position changes over time.
## Displacement
  
  * **Definition:**  Change in position.
  * **Formula:** $\Delta x = x_f - x_i$ (where $\Delta x$ is displacement, $x_f$ is final position, and $x_i$ is initial position).
  * **Positive or negative:**  Indicates direction (positive for movement in the positive x-direction, negative for movement in the negative x-direction).
  * **Distance vs. displacement:** Displacement is not the same as the total distance traveled.
## Speed and Velocity
  
  * **Instantaneous speed:**  The speed at a particular moment. (Example: "I am currently going at 25 mph")
  * **Instantaneous velocity:**  Speed with direction. (Example: "I am currently going at 25 mph North on Pine Street")
  * **Average speed:** Total distance traveled divided by the total time taken. (Example: "I drove the 60 miles in one hour")
## Average Velocity
  
  * **Definition:**  Displacement divided by the time interval.
  * **Formula:** $v_x = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$ (where $v_x$ is average velocity, $\Delta x$ is displacement, and $\Delta t$ is the time interval).
  * **Units:** meters per second (m/s)
  * **Subscript x:**  Indicates velocity along the x-axis.
  * **Positive or negative:**  $v_x > 0$ means movement in the positive x-direction, $v_x < 0$ means movement in the negative x-direction.
## Average Velocity and x-t Graph
  
  * **Slope:** The slope of a line connecting two points on a position-time (x-t) graph represents the average velocity between those times.
## Instantaneous Velocity
  
  * **Tangent line:**  On an x-t graph, the instantaneous velocity at a specific time is the slope of the line tangent to the curve at that point.
  * **Calculus:**  Instantaneous velocity is the derivative of position with respect to time ($v_x = \frac{dx}{dt}$).
## Speedometer
  
  * **Absolute value:**  A speedometer shows the absolute value of instantaneous velocity, which is always positive.
  * **Speed:** Speed ($v$) is the magnitude of velocity: $v = |v_x|$
## Direction of Velocity
  
  * **$v_x > 0$:** Object moves in the positive x-direction.
  * **$v_x < 0$:** Object moves in the negative x-direction.
## Acceleration
  
  * **Definition:**  How quickly velocity changes over time (time rate of change of velocity).
  * **Formula:** $a_x = \frac{dv_x}{dt} = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2}$  (where $a_x$ is acceleration, $v_x$ is velocity, and $t$ is time).
  * **Units:** meters per second squared (m/s<sup>2</sup>)
  * **Slope:**  Acceleration is the slope of a velocity vs. time ($v_x$ vs. $t$) graph.
  * **Change in velocity:**  Acceleration produces a change in velocity ($dv_x = a_x dt$).
## Signs of Acceleration and Velocity
  
  * **$a_x > 0$:**  
    * If $v_x > 0$, the object speeds up.
    * If $v_x < 0$, the object slows down.
  * **$a_x < 0$:**  
    * If $v_x > 0$, the object slows down.
    * If $v_x < 0$, the object speeds up.
