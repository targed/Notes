## Frictional Forces
  
  * **Definition:** A frictional force is a force that opposes motion or impending motion relative to a surface.
  * **Causes:**  Frictional forces are caused by the microscopic interactions between two surfaces in contact, including irregularities and molecular adhesion.
  * **Direction:** Frictional forces always act parallel to the surface.
  * **Types:**
    * **Static friction:**  Prevents motion from starting.
    * **Kinetic friction:** Opposes motion in progress.
## Static Friction
  
  * **Static friction vector ($ \vec{f}_s$):** Directed opposite to the direction of motion an object **would** have relative to the surface **if no friction were present**.
  * **Determining direction:** 
    1. Imagine the object without friction.
    2. Determine the direction in which it would move due to the applied force.
    3. The static friction force acts in the opposite direction.
  * **Magnitude:**
    *  $|\vec{f}_s| = |\vec{F}_{app}|$ until the maximum static friction is reached.
## Maximum Static Friction
  
  * **Maximum static friction:** The maximum force that static friction can exert before an object starts to move.
  * **Formula:** $f_{s_{max}} = \mu_s N$ 
    * $\mu_s$: **Coefficient of static friction**, a positive number that depends on the materials in contact. 
    * $N$: Magnitude of the normal force.
  * **Inequality:**  The static friction force can range from 0 to the maximum value: $f_s \leq f_{s_{max}} = \mu_s N$.
## Getting an Object to Move
  
  * **Overcoming static friction:** When the applied force exceeds the maximum static friction ($|\vec{F}_{app}| > f_{s_{max}} = \mu_s N$), the object begins to move.
  * **Graph:** A graph of friction force vs. applied force shows a linear increase in static friction until it reaches the maximum value, then drops to a constant kinetic friction force once the object is moving.
  
  **Demo:** Demonstrating the coefficient of static friction ($\mu_s$) for a truck on a horizontal surface.
## Kinetic Friction
  
  * **Kinetic friction vector ($\vec{f}_k$):**  Acts opposite to the direction of velocity relative to the surface.
  * **Formula:**  $f_k = \mu_k N$
    * $\mu_k$: **Coefficient of kinetic friction**, a positive number that depends on the materials in contact (typically smaller than $\mu_s$).
    * $N$: Magnitude of the normal force.
## Static and Kinetic Friction: Summary
  
  * **Static friction:**  Adjusts its magnitude to prevent motion until it reaches its maximum value ($f_{s_{max}} = \mu_s N$).
  * **Kinetic friction:** Has a constant magnitude ($f_k = \mu_k N$) once an object is in motion.

## Discussion Question
  
  **Problem:** A block of mass $m$ is at rest on an incline that makes an angle $\theta$ with the horizontal. The coefficient of static friction between the block and the incline is $\mu_s$. What is the magnitude of the frictional force, in terms of system parameters?
  
  **Options:**
  
  * A) $\mu_s N$
  * B) $\mu_s mg$
  * C) $\mu_s mg \cos \theta$
  * D) $mg \sin \theta$
  
  **Answer:**  D) $mg \sin \theta$. Since the block is at rest, the static friction force must balance the component of the weight force acting parallel to the incline.
