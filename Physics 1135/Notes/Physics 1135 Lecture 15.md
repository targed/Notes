## Introduction
  
  This lecture introduces the physics of static fluids (fluids at rest), covering:
  
  * Pressure
  * Pascal's Principle
  * Buoyancy force
## Pressure
  
  * **Definition:** Pressure ($p$) is the force magnitude per unit area.
  * **Formula:** $p = \frac{dF}{dA}$
  * **Units:** Pascal (Pa) = N/m²
  * **Fluid at rest:**
    * **Isotropic pressure:** At a given depth, the pressure is the same in all directions.
    * **Perpendicular force:** The force due to pressure is always perpendicular to any surface it acts upon.
## Pressure Increase with Depth
  
  * **Cause:** Pressure in a fluid increases with depth due to the weight of the fluid above.
  * **Derivation:** Consider a cylindrical column of fluid with height $h$ and cross-sectional area $A$.
    * Weight of the fluid column: $W = mg = \rho Vg = \rho Ahg$ (where $\rho$ is the density of the fluid)
    * Pressure difference: $\Delta p = \frac{W}{A} = \frac{\rho Ahg}{A} = \rho gh$
  * **Formula:** $p_{below} - p_{above} = \rho gh$
## Atmospheric Pressure
  
  * **Atmospheric pressure ($p_{atm}$):** The pressure exerted by the Earth's atmosphere.
  * **Value:** $p_{atm} \approx 100 \text{ kPa} = 10^5 \text{ N/m}^2$
  * **Force due to atmospheric pressure:**  Consider a 1 cm x 1 cm area. The force due to atmospheric pressure on this area is approximately 10 N (equivalent to the weight of a 1 kg mass).  For a larger area, like 10 cm x 10 cm (above our heads), the force is approximately 1000 N (equivalent to the weight of a 100 kg mass).
  
  **Demo:** Magdeburg hemispheres demonstrate the significant force exerted by atmospheric pressure.
## Magdeburg Hemispheres
  
  * **Historical experiment:** Otto von Guericke, 1654, demonstrated the power of atmospheric pressure by creating a near-vacuum inside two hemispheres and showing that two teams of horses could not pull them apart.
  
  **(Image or video of Magdeburg hemispheres experiment)**
  
  * **Calculation:** For hemispheres with a diameter of 50 cm, the force required to separate them is roughly 20,000 N (equivalent to the weight of a 2000 kg mass). In a classroom demonstration with 10 cm diameter hemispheres and an imperfect vacuum, the force is about 800 N.
## Pascal's Principle
  
  * **Statement:** Pressure applied to a confined fluid increases the pressure throughout the fluid by the same amount.
  * **Consequence:** All points at the same level in a contiguous (connected) fluid have the same pressure.
## Applications of Pascal's Principle
  
  * **Hydraulic lift:** Uses Pascal's principle to multiply force. A small force applied to a small area creates a pressure increase that is transmitted to a larger area, resulting in a larger force.
  * **Formula:** $\Delta p = \frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2}$

## The Longest Straw
  
  **Question:** How high can you pump water by suction (creating a vacuum above the water)?
  
  * **Explanation:** The atmospheric pressure pushes the water up the straw. The pressure difference between the atmosphere and the vacuum above the water column equals $\rho gh$.
  * **Formula:**  $p_{atm} - 0 = \rho_{water} gh_{max}$
  * **Maximum height:**  $h_{max} = \frac{p_{atm}}{\rho_{water}g} \approx 10 \text{ m}$

## Buoyancy and Archimedes' Principle
  
  * **Statement:** An object fully or partially submerged in a fluid experiences an upward buoyancy force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the object.
  * **Formula:** $B = \rho_{fluid} V_{disp} g$
    * $B$: Buoyancy force
    * $\rho_{fluid}$: Density of the fluid
    * $V_{disp}$: Volume of fluid displaced by the object
    * $g$: Acceleration due to gravity
  
  **(Diagram illustrating Archimedes' principle with a submerged object and the displaced fluid.)**
## Consequences of Archimedes' Principle
  
  * **Floating:**  If the density of the object is less than the density of the fluid, the buoyancy force will be greater than the object's weight, causing it to float.
  * **Sinking:**  If the density of the object is greater than the density of the fluid, the buoyancy force will be less than the object's weight, causing it to sink.
  
  **Demo:** Demonstrating buoyancy force using objects of different densities in water.
## Buoyancy Force and Scale Reading
  
  **(Diagrams showing a scale with a container of water and objects of different densities. The scale reading changes due to the buoyancy force.)**
  
  * **Object not in water:** $F_s = W$ (Scale reading equals the weight of the object)
  * **Object partially submerged:** $F_s = W - B$ (Scale reading equals the weight minus the buoyancy force)
  * **Object fully submerged (but not touching the bottom):** $F_s = W - B$ (Same as partially submerged)
