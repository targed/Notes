## Brief History of Cosmology
  
  * **Ptolemy (85-165 AD):** Geocentric theory (Earth at the center of the universe).
  * **Nicolaus Copernicus (1473-1543):** Heliocentric theory (Sun at the center of the solar system).
  * **Tycho Brahe (1546-1601):**  Made precise astronomical observations that provided crucial data for Kepler.
  * **Johannes Kepler (1571-1630):** Developed Kepler's laws of planetary motion based on Brahe's data.
  * **Galileo Galilei (1564-1642):**  Used the telescope to make observations supporting the heliocentric model, such as the moons orbiting Jupiter and the phases of Venus.

### 2nd Law (Law of Equal Areas)
  
  A line drawn between the Sun and a planet sweeps out equal areas in equal intervals of time.

### 3rd Law (Law of Harmonies)
  
  The square of a planet's orbital period ($T^2$) is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis length ($a^3$) of its orbit.
  
  * $T^2 \propto a^3$
  * $\frac{T_A^2}{T_B^2} = \frac{a_A^3}{a_B^3}$  (for two planets A and B)

## Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation
  
  * **Statement:** Every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force that is proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centers.
  * **Formula:** $F_{grav} = \frac{GmM}{r^2}$
    * $F_{grav}$: Gravitational force
    * $G$: Universal gravitational constant ($G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N m}^2/\text{kg}^2$)
    * $m$ and $M$: Masses of the two objects
    * $r$: Distance between their centers
  * **Action-reaction pair:** The gravitational forces between two objects form an action-reaction pair (equal in magnitude and opposite in direction).
## Gravitation Near Earth's Surface
  
  * **Gravitational force:**  Near Earth's surface, the gravitational force on an object of mass $m$ is:
    * $F_{grav} = \frac{GmM_E}{R_E^2}$ 
    * where $M_E$ is the mass of Earth and $R_E$ is the radius of Earth.
  * **Weight:**  We also know that the weight of an object near Earth's surface is $W = mg$.
  * **Relationship between $g$ and $G$:**  Comparing the two expressions for the gravitational force, we find:
    * $mg = \frac{GmM_E}{R_E^2}$
    * $g = \frac{GM_E}{R_E^2}$
  * **Free-fall acceleration on any planet:**  This formula can be generalized to find the free-fall acceleration ($g$) on the surface of any planet with mass $M$ and radius $R$:
    * $g = \frac{GM}{R^2}$
## Finding the Mass of Earth
  
  If we know the values of $g$, $G$, and $R_E$, we can find the mass of Earth:
  
  * $M_E = \frac{gR_E^2}{G}$
  
  * **Measuring G:**  The Cavendish balance was used to measure the gravitational constant $G$.
  * **Measuring $R_E$:** Eratosthenes measured the radius of Earth around 200 BC using a clever geometric method involving the shadow cast by the Sun at different locations.
## Satellite Motion
  
  * **Centripetal force:** For a satellite of mass $m_{sat}$ orbiting a planet of mass $M$ in a circular orbit of radius $R$, the gravitational force provides the centripetal force:
    * $\sum F_x = F_{grav,x} = m_{sat}a_x = m_{sat}(+a_c)$
    * $\frac{Gm_{sat}M}{R^2} = m_{sat}\frac{v^2}{R}$
    * $\frac{GM}{R} = v^2 = \left(\frac{2\pi R}{T}\right)^2$
  * **Kepler's 3rd Law (derived):** Solving for the period $T$, we get Kepler's 3rd Law:
    * $T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM}R^3$
