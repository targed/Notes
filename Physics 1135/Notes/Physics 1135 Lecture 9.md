## Concepts Covered
### Kinematics
  
  * **Definitions of kinematics quantities:** Position, displacement, velocity, acceleration
  * **Relationships between kinematics quantities:** 
    * Velocity as the derivative of position
    * Acceleration as the derivative of velocity
  * **Interpreting graphs:**  Position-time graphs, velocity-time graphs
  * **Concepts of projectile motion:** 
    * Independence of horizontal and vertical motion
    * Constant horizontal velocity
    * Constant vertical acceleration due to gravity
  * **Problem types:** 1-dimensional and 2-dimensional problems
### Forces
  
  * **Newton's 2nd Law:** 
    * Relationship between force, mass, and acceleration: $\sum \vec{F} = m\vec{a}$
    * Component form: $\sum F_x = ma_x$, $\sum F_y = ma_y$
  * **Newton's 3rd Law:** Action-reaction pairs
  * **Coupled objects:** Systems with multiple objects connected by ropes or strings.
  * **Friction:** 
    * Static friction: Prevents motion from starting, $f_s \leq f_{s_{max}} = \mu_s N$
    * Kinetic friction: Opposes motion in progress, $f_k = \mu_k N$
  * **Centripetal acceleration:**  Acceleration towards the center of a circular path, $a_c = \frac{v^2}{R}$.
  * **Problem types:**  Problems with one or multiple objects, including problems involving friction and circular motion.
## Tips for Multiple-Choice Questions
  
  * **Identify the concept:** Determine the main physics concept being tested in the question.
  * **Recall knowledge:**  Review what you know about that concept, including relevant equations and problem-solving strategies.
  * **Answer open-ended first:**  If possible, try to answer the question without looking at the answer choices. Then, select the choice that best matches your answer.
  * **Use diagrams and equations:**  Sketching a diagram or working out a few lines of equations can often help you visualize the problem and eliminate incorrect answers.

## Summary of Litany for Kinematics Problems
  
  1. **Draw a sketch:** Represent the physical situation visually.
  2. **Draw and label vectors:**  Include initial velocity, acceleration, and any other relevant vectors.
  3. **Draw coordinate system:**  Choose an appropriate coordinate system with an origin.
  4. **Indicate initial and final positions:** Mark the starting and ending points of the motion along the axes.
  5. **Start with an OSE:**  Choose the relevant "Official Starting Equation" from the kinematics equations.
  6. **Replace generic quantities:** Substitute the specific values and components given in the problem.
  7. **Solve algebraically:** Solve the equation for the desired quantity.

## Summary of Litany for Force Problems
  
  1. **Draw a sketch:**  Represent the physical situation visually.
  2. **Draw a free-body diagram:** Show all the forces acting on the object of interest.
  3. **Label each element:**  Clearly label all forces and other relevant quantities.
  4. **Draw a coordinate system:**  Choose an appropriate coordinate system, often aligning one axis with the direction of acceleration.
  5. **Draw force components:** Resolve any forces not parallel to the axes into their components.
  6. **Choose an OSE:**  Select the relevant "Official Starting Equation" (usually Newton's second law in component form: $\sum F_x = ma_x$ or $\sum F_y = ma_y$).
  7. **Write out force components:**  Express the sum of the force components in each direction explicitly.
  8. **Solve algebraically:** Solve the equations for the desired quantity.

## Things to Remember for Circular Motion
  
  * **Centripetal acceleration:**
    * Formula: $a_c = \frac{v^2}{R}$
    * Direction: Always towards the center of the circle.
    * Cause:  A net force directed towards the center of the circle (centripetal force).
  * **Setting up force problems:**
    * Choose one axis of your coordinate system to point towards the center of the circle.
