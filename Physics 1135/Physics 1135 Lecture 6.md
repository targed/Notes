## Newton's 3rd Law
  
  * **Common version:** For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. This version, while widely known, can be misleading because it suggests a sequential order of action and reaction. 
  * **Better version:**  If object A exerts a force $\vec{F}_{AB}$ on object B, then object B exerts a force $\vec{F}_{BA}$ on object A, with $\vec{F}_{BA} = -\vec{F}_{AB}$.
  * **Key points:**
    * Forces always occur in pairs.
    * The two forces in an action-reaction pair are equal in magnitude but opposite in direction.
    * Each force in the pair acts on a different object.
## Action-Reaction Pairs
  
  * **Example:**  Consider a person pushing a box.
    * The person exerts a force on the box (action).
    * The box exerts an equal and opposite force on the person (reaction).
  * **Free-body diagrams:**  Action-reaction pairs are never shown on the same free-body diagram because they act on different objects.
## Example: Cat at Rest on a Table
  
  * **Forces:**  A cat is at rest on a table. The forces acting on the cat are:
    * $\vec{N}$: Normal force from the table, pushing upward on the cat.
    * $\vec{W}$: Weight of the cat, pulling downward due to gravity.
  * **Newton's 2nd law:** Since the cat is at rest, its acceleration is zero ($\vec{a} = 0$). Therefore, the net force on the cat is zero: $\vec{N} = -\vec{W}$.
  * **Action-reaction pairs:**
    * The weight of the cat ($\vec{W}$) and the gravitational force exerted by the cat on the Earth are an action-reaction pair.
    * The normal force from the table ($\vec{N}$) and the force exerted by the cat on the table are an action-reaction pair.
  * **Different objects:** It's important to remember that the two forces in an action-reaction pair act on **different** objects.
## Object on Inclined Plane
  
  * **Normal force:** The normal force is always perpendicular to the surface.
  * **Weight:** The weight force always acts vertically downward.
  * **Choosing axes:** When dealing with inclined planes, it's usually convenient to choose a coordinate system where one axis is parallel to the incline and the other axis is perpendicular to the incline.
  * **Components of weight:** The weight force can be resolved into components parallel and perpendicular to the incline.
    * $W_x = +W \cos \theta = +Mg \cos \theta$ (parallel to incline)
    * $W_y = -W \sin \theta = -Mg \sin \theta$ (perpendicular to incline)
  * **Caution:** Do not memorize these formulas! Understand how to derive them based on the geometry of the inclined plane and the direction of the weight vector.

## Coupled Objects: Ropes and Pulleys
  
  * **Assumptions:** We often make the following approximations:
    * **Massless, un-stretchable rope:** This implies that the tension is constant throughout the rope.
    * **Massless, frictionless pulley:** This means the tension remains constant as the rope passes over the pulley.
  * **Caution:** These approximations are not always valid. If the mass and spatial extension of the pulley are significant, the tension will not remain constant.
