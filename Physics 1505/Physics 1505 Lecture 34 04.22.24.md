# Active galaxy nuclei, radio galaxies, and supermassive black holes
## Galaxies evolve over time
- The future the object; the younger it appears
- The look back time is what it looked like in the past
- Irregular galaxies form in the early universe
- Galaxies form from over dense regions in the universe
## Galactic interactions
- The relative distances between galaxies is not that large when compared to the distances between stars
- Galaxies tend to interact with other galaxies more than the stars within them
- Galaxies interact pretty frequently
- Galaxies can merge and get distorted
- While the stars in the galaxy do not collide, the gas does
- The merger of galaxies triggers a lot of star formation
## Galaxy interactions
- Early galaxies were irregular and interacted pretty frequently
- When galaxies merge, the stability of each gets disrupted and star formation increases in the arms
- Collisions may explain why elliptical galaxies tend to be found where galaxies are close together
## Gas cycles in galaxies
- Galaxies seem to be divided into two classes, blue galaxies and red galaxies
- Galaxies go through a cycle as they age
  - Galaxies start as star forming systems, appearing blue in color
  - Galaxy mergers crate larger and more luminous galaxies with less star formation, appearing red in color
## Starburst galaxies
- Some galaxies are called starburst galaxies which produce stars at a very high rate
- Starbursts galaxies have a lot of black holes because of the collapse of many massive stars
## Gas outflows
- The intensity of supernova explosions in starburst Galaxy is can drive intergalactic winds
- Galactic wind in small galaxies can drive away most of its gas
## Active galaxies
- If the center of a galaxy is unusually bright, we call it an active galactic nucleus
- The most luminous examples are called quasars
- The highly redshifted spectra of quasar indicate large distances
- Variability shows that all this energy comes from a region smaller than our solar system
- the luminosity of quasars can be greater than a trillion times our sun
## Radio galaxies
- Radio galaxies contain active nuclei, shooting out vast jets of plasma that emits radian waves coming from electrons that move near the speed of light
- The lobes of radio galaxies can extend over hundreds of thousands of light years
- Radio galaxies are not as active as quasars
- An active galactic nucleus can shoot out blobs of plasma, moving it, nearly the speed of light
- This suggest a profound influence on the surrounding a galaxy
- Radio galaxies are just quasars in a different portion of their life cycle
- The supermassive black holes power these galaxies
- The black holes form accretion discs that get hot and shoot out matter
## Supermassive black holes
- Speed and distance of gas orbiting the center of galaxy M87 indicative black hole with a massive at least 2 to 3 billion suns
- Orbits of stars and direct imaging indicates a mass closer to 6 billion masses of the sun
- You can figure out how far away supermassive black holes are by measuring their redshift
- We have “imaged” black holes by looking their accretion disks
