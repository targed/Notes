# Parsec
- In order to measure accurately, a new unit was developed called parsecs
  - A parsec is the distance to a star for which the star has a parallax angle equal to 1 arc second
  - The greater the parallax, the closer it is. The smaller the parallax, the further away it is
  - Need to use radians

# Astrometry
- A branch of astronomy that involves precise measurements of the positions and movements of stars and other celestial bodies.
  - Around 190 BC: Hipparchus measures the positions of 850 stars.
  - Around 150 AD: Ptolemy included a catalog of 1,022 stars in the Almagest.
  - 15th century: Ulugh Beg measures 1,019 stars with a precision of approximately 20 arcminutes.
  - 16th century: Tycho Brahemeasures star positions with a precision of 15-35 arcsec
  - 1807: Friedrich Bessel makes the first measurement of stellar parallax: 0.3 arcsec for the binary star 61 Cygni.
  - End of 19th century: about 60 stellar parallaxes are known.
  - 20 century: Use of photographic plates and CCD cameras reduced optical uncertainties to about one milliarsecond: A few thousand star parallaxes are measured
  - 1989-1993, the European Space Agency's Hipparcos satellite measures positions, parallaxes, and proper motions of 118,218 stars.
  - 2013: The ESA's Gaia satellite is launched.
    - Largest digital camera ever sent into space
    - Measured around 1.46 Billion stars with a precision of up to 0.1 milliarcsecond

- Hallmarks of science

  - Observations for phenomena must be as simple as possible

  - Science makes testable predictions that can be tested
    - If predictions do not agree with observations, the. The model must be revised
