- When bodies orbit, they tug on other bodies
- We can detect this wobble and figure out the masses and orbits of all the planets
- We can detect planets by measuring the change in a stars position in the sky
- The tiny movements are very hard to measure
- You can use the Doppler effect

- An object that is moving further away from you has a longer wavelength while an object that is moving closer to you, it has a shorter wavelength

- Measuring a stars Doppler shift can tell its motion towards and away from us
- Doppler shifts tell us about a planets mass and the shape of its orbit
  - From the height of the curves, you can tell the mass, orbit, and more about the planet
- You look at how the lines on the spectrum wobble. Using that wobble, you can use it to find the v of star and thus the mass.
- This technique was used to discover the first exoplanet
- The velocity is positive and negative on a pre defined period which is the orbital period of the planet
  - 51 Pegasi has the first exoplanet ever discovered
  - It showed a planet with the mass of Jupiter orbiting the star every 4 days which challenged our model of the solar system
## Transit eclipse
- A transit is when a planet crosses in front of a star
- An eclipse is also sometimes seen when the planet passes behind the star
- You see less light when the planet passes in front of the star
- By measuring the variation of the light from the star, you can tell if there is a planet
- NASA launched Kepler to look for transiting planets
  - It was able to measure the 0.008% decline in brightness when an earth mass planet passes in front of a sun like star
  - The mission found over 4500 planets
## Spectrum during transit
- For a moment during the eclipse of a planet in front of a star, the light passing through the atmosphere allows us to tell many things about that planet
