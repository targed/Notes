# Life in the solar system
## When did life arise on earth
- Life probably arose around 3.85 billion years ago
- Using carbon dating and fossils, we can determine how life was in the past
- Life started very slowly
- Life started with unicellular organisms around hydrothermal vents
## Chemicals to life
- We don’t quite know how life came to be
- In the beginning of earth, there were complex organic molecules floating around and then (we assume) they eventually formed life
## Necessities for life
- A nutrient source
- Energy
  - From the sun, chemical reactions, and internal heat
- Liquid water
  - Needed to combine chemicals together
## Habitable planets
- A habitable world contains the basic necessities for life including liquid water
  - That does not mean it had life
- It requires a star system that are old enough for revolution to happen, need to have stable orbits, and have a habitable zone
- Even with these constraints, there are probably billions of stars in the Milky Way with habitable worlds
- The habitable zone is the zone in which a planet can contain liquid water and support life
- The more massive the star, the larger its habitable zone
- There are 4 ingredients for habitability
  - Habitable zone
  - Volcanism
    - Atmosphere and oceans
  - Plate tectonics
    - Carbon dioxide cycle
  - Planetary magnetic field
    - Protects against radiation
- You can tell what a planets atmosphere is made of by a spectrum analysis
- They may have been life on Venus in the past
  - However, Venus’s atmosphere is mainly made of co2 and very very hot
## The greenhouse effect
- Visible light passes through the atmosphere and warms the planet’s surface
- The atmosphere absorbs reflected light from the surface
- All atmospheres trap heat to different degrees
- Some greenhouse effect is good
