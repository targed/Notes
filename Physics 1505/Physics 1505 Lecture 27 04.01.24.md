# Star formation
- Heavier elements are possibly formed by the merger of neutron stars
## Where and why do stars form
- Stars form in dark clouds of dusty gas in interstellar space
- The gas between stars is called the interstellar medium
- We can determine the composition of interstellar gas from its absorption lines on the spectra of stars
  - The interstellar gas is made up of 70% hydrogen, 28% helium, and 2% other heavier elements
- Interstellar gas can be composed of atoms, ions, or molecules
  - At colder temperatures, molecules can form.
  - As the template is raised, the molecules beam apart into atoms and as they get hotter, they form into ions
- Astronomers calm everything that is not hydrogen and helium “metals”
## Molecular clouds
- Most of the mater in star forming clouds is in the form of molecules
- These molecular clouds have a temp of 10-30K and a density of about 300 molecules per cubic cm
- Most of what we know about molecular clouds comes from observing the mason lines of carbon monoxide
## Interstellar dust
- Interstate dust are tiny solid particles that block our view of stars on the other side of the cloud
- These particles are less than 1 micrometer in size
- Interstellar dust easily combines together to form complex molecules

## Star formation
- ![2024-04-01-15-30-12.jpeg](../assets/2024-04-01-15-30-12.jpeg)
- You have to have a gas cloud that has about 50 times the mass of the sun that collapses. They are usually light years across
- Random motion in the cloud causes some regions to become more dense than others
- The large cloud fragments into smaller lumps of matter
- Each lump can form 1 or 2 new stars
## Gravity vs pressure
- ![2024-04-01-15-33-34.jpeg](../assets/2024-04-01-15-33-34.jpeg)
- Gravity creates stars only if it can overcome the force of the thermal pressure in a cloud
- Emission lines from a molecule cloud can prevent a pressure buildup by converting infrared to radio and photons
- ![2024-04-01-15-35-03.jpeg](../assets/2024-04-01-15-35-03.jpeg)
- The smaller the “chunk” of gas, the less heat it retains and the les thermal pressure it has
- By fragmenting the cloud, gravity can take over
## cloud fragmentation
- ![2024-04-01-15-38-51.jpeg](../assets/2024-04-01-15-38-51.jpeg)
- If and only if the cloud is very small then you can have single star formation
- In general, the clouds form clusters of stars
## The first stars
- When the first stars formed in the universe from the primordial interstellar gas, elements like carbon and oxygen had not yet been made
- Without CO molecules to provide cooling, the clouds were very warm
- The fist stars must have been very massive, had short lives, and went supernova
