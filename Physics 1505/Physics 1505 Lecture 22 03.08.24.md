# Stars
## Stellar properties
- ![2024-03-08-15-02-59.jpeg](../assets/2024-03-08-15-02-59.jpeg)
- Through spectroscopy, we can tell the chemical composition of a star
- The color ad spectral type can tell us the temperature of the star
- Most stars are in binary pairs. Not through binary pairs can we get an accurate measurement of a stars mass
- Stars luminosity spans a lot. From 1/10000 the luminosity of the sun to 1000000 times the luminosity of the sun
- Stars temperature does vary a little. 3000K to 50000K
- The sun is in the middle of the scale for most parameters
## The Hertzsprung-Russel diagram
- ![2024-03-08-15-08-13.jpeg](../assets/2024-03-08-15-08-13.jpeg)
- Stars temperature vs its luminosity
- The line of stars is called the main sequence and shows that there is a relationship between luminosity and temperature
- The scale is logarithmic so it goes up or down by 10 times
- The most luminous stars are more massive and are hotter. The biggest ones are called blue giants
- The least luminous stars are small and are cooler. The smallest ones are called brown dwarves
- The brighter the star burns, the shorter its lifecycle is
- There are some stars that don’t fit in the diagram. There are “islands of stability” in which some stars can be in
- White dwarves are small stars that burn very hot
- Supergiants are big stars that burn cool but emit a lot of light
- Bernard’s star was one of the first stars to be discovered that did not fit the diagram so it was discarded. Don’t throw away datapoints
- ![2024-03-08-15-20-42.jpeg](../assets/2024-03-08-15-20-42.jpeg)
- The sun is a main sequence star
- Luminous main sequence stars are hot (blue)
- Less luminous ones are cooler (red)
## Mass
- Mass is the most fundamental property that influences most of the other properties
- ![2024-03-08-15-23-52.jpeg](../assets/2024-03-08-15-23-52.jpeg)
- Depending on the mass of a star, the star may have a different structure
- Small stars do not have a radiative zone
- Massive stars have a large radiative zone
- The mass of the star is directly proportional to the lifetime of the star
- Large stars burn up faster than smaller ones

## Giants, supergiants, and white dwarfs
- ![2024-03-08-15-32-53.jpeg](../assets/2024-03-08-15-32-53.jpeg)
- Stellar properties depend both on mass and age. Those that have finished rising hydrogen into helium in their cores are no longer on the main sequence
- All stars become larger and reset after exhausting their core hydrogen supply
- We can compare how stars sustain themselves after nuclear fusion has subsided. 
  observations of star clusters shows that a star becomes larger, redder, and more luminous after its time on the main sequence is over
- Stars “evolve” off the main sequence
- This is because the star starts collapsing in itself increasing nuclear fusion and making it more luminous… at least for a little while
- Eventually the star cannot produce enough energy and becomes a red giant
- ![2024-03-08-15-41-13.jpeg](../assets/2024-03-08-15-41-13.jpeg)
- Red giants are caused because as a star collapses the temperature in the core eventually gets hot enough to fuse helium
