## What happens when fusion end?
- After core helium fusion stops, helium fuses into carbon in a shell aground the carbon core, and hydrogen fuses into helium in a shell around a helium layer
- This double shell fusion stage never reaches equilibrium. The fusion rate periodically spikes upwards in a series of thermal pulses
- With each spike, convection dredges carbon up from the core and transports it to the surface
- After the star runs out of helium, the star starts collapsing and starts fusing carbon
- Carbon is very stable and thus it is very hard for the star to fuse carbon
- Basically the star has an inert carbon core with a helium shell inside a carbon shell inside a hydrogen shell
- For a while, you get a pulsating star. that when it is bigger, it is brighter and when it is smaller, it is dimmer
- We observe the late stages of low mass stars before they become white dwarfs as variable stars
## What are variable stars?
- Variable stars vary in brightness because they cannot achieve proper balance between power welling up from the core and power radiated from the surface
- Such a star expands and contacts, trying to find equilibrium while not succeeding
- These variable stars have brightness cycle
- Cepheid variable stars
  - Most pulsating variable stars inhabit an instability strip on the HR diagram
  - The most luminous of these stars are known as Cepheid variables
## Planetary nebulae
- If the variable stars don’t reach equilibrium, they continue to mix until helium gets compressed to where it fuses again.
- This happens in such a short amount of time and it releases so much energy that the stars outer layers get ejected into space.
- This creates a planetary nebula that are made of the materials from the last act of a dying star. They are made of hydrogen, helium, and carbon
- The core of carbon still remains
## White dwarf
- Fusion progresses no further in a low mass star because the core temperature never grows hot enough for the fusion of heavier elements
- Degeneracy pressure supports the white dwarf against gravity
- It is a solid star that cools down over time
- The sun will undergo the transition to a white dwarf
  - When it does that, the suns luminosity will rise to 1000 times or current level which will make earth uninhabitable
- White dwarfs are the remaining cores of dead stars
- White dwarf stars have a radius about the size of earth
- White dwarfs cool off and grow dimmer over time
- The density of white dwarfs is around 1000000 g/cm^3
- A white dwarf has a diamond carbon core covered by other elements
- We can measure the cooling time of white dwarfs
  - ![2024-03-11-15-49-42.jpeg](../assets/2024-03-11-15-49-42.jpeg)
- White dwarfs are used to measure the age of the universe
## The Chandrasekhar limit
- Quantum mechanics says that electrons must move faster when they are squeezed into a very small space
- As a white dwarfs mass approaches 1.4 times the mass of the sun, its electrons must move near the speed of light
- Because nothing can move faster than the speed of light, a white dwarf calamity be more massive than 1.4 times the mass of the sun
## Accretion discs
- Mass falling towards the white dwarf from its binary companion has some angular momentum
- The matter therefore orbits the white dwarf in an accretion disc
- Friction between orbiting rings of matter in the disk transfers angular momentum outward and causes the disk to heat up
