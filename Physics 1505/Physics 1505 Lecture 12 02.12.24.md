# Terrestrial planets
- Rocky, small, and close to the sun
- The average density is above 3-4 grams per cubic cm
# Earths interior
- Earth has a core of high density, pressure, and temperature made of nickel to iron
- Earth has a mantle with moderate density, pressure, and temperature. Mostly made of silicon and oxygen
- Earth has a crust that is the lowest density, pressure, and temperature. Mostly made of granite and basalt
- We know the structure of the earth because of seismic waves
# Seismic waves
- Seismic waves that travel through earth tell us what it is like in the inside
  - P waves push matter back and fourth
  - S waves shake matter side to side
- Waves propagate as they encounter different densities throughout earth
- The liquid outer core bends P waves but stops S waves
- Seismic waves are often called earthquakes
- The earthquake scale is logarithmic so each magnitude is 10 times the previous one
# Other planet interiors
- Most other terrestrial planets have about the same composition as earth
- Mercury has a higher density and is smaller so it has a larger core with a thinner crust
- Mars has a lower density so it has a smaller core with a thicker crust
- Differentiation is where the heavy material in a planets core is pulled towards the center
- Besides earthquakes, there are other means of measuring earths interior
- We can measure using geological processes, pressure measurements, temperature measurements, and isotope measurements
- Convection is the transport of heat through the transport of matter. Warm rises, cool sinks
- Conduction transfers heat from hot material to cold
- Radiation transfers energy through space

# Surfaces
- There are 4 processes that shape the surface of a planet
  - Impacts by asteroids or comets
    - Most cratering happened very soon after the solar system formed
    - A surface with many craters has not changed in a long time
    - Craters are about 10 times as wide as the objects that made them
  - Plate tectonics
    - Convection in the mantle creates stresses on the crust called tectonic forces
    - Compression of crust plates creates mountains
    - Pulling apart the crust plates creates fissures
  - Erosion
    - Erosion is an atmospheric process that break down and transport rock
    - Can only happen if a planet has an atmosphere
    - Planets that are too close to the sun are to hot for water and thus have less erosion
    - Planets that are too far from the sun are too cold for rain which limits erosion
    - If a planet spins quickly, it has more erosion and a stronger magnetic field
  - Volcanism
    - When magma finds it way through the crust to the surface
    - Thick lava makes steep stratovolcanoes
    - Thinner lava makes broad shield volcanoes
    - Runny lava makes lava planes
    - Volcanoes also release gas into the atmosphere
