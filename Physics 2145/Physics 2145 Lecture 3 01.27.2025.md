# Lecture 3: Electric Field
## Review: Coulomb's Law
  
  Recall Coulomb's Law, which describes the force between two point charges:
  
  $F = k \frac{|q_1||q_2|}{r^2}$
  
  *   $F$: Magnitude of the electrostatic force
  *   $k$: Coulomb's constant (9 x 10<sup>9</sup> N•m<sup>2</sup>/C<sup>2</sup>)
  *   $q_1$, $q_2$: Magnitudes of the charges
  *   $r$: Distance between the charges
  
  **Key Interactions:**
  
  *   Like charges repel.
  *   Unlike charges attract.
## The Concept of an Electric Field
  
  Charged particles exert forces on each other even when separated by a distance. To explain this "action at a distance," we introduce the concept of an **electric field**.
  
  **Explanation:**
  
  1. A charged particle creates an **electric field** in the space surrounding it.
  2. Other charged particles within this field experience a force due to the field's presence.
  
  **Electric Field (E):**
  
  *   Defined as the force per unit charge at a given point in space.
  *   **Units:** Newtons per Coulomb (N/C)
  *   **Vector Quantity:** Possesses both magnitude and direction. The electric field is a **vector field**, meaning that it exists at every point in space.
## Electric Field of a Point Charge
  
  Consider a point charge *q* creating an electric field. If we place a small test charge *q'* at a distance *r* from *q*, the test charge will experience a force given by Coulomb's Law:
  
  $F = k \frac{|q||q'|}{r^2}$
  
  The electric field *E* due to *q* at the location of *q'* is:
  
  $E = \frac{F}{q'} = k \frac{|q|}{r^2}$
  
  **Direction of the Electric Field:**
  
  *   **Positive Charge (q > 0):** The electric field points radially **away** from the positive charge.
  *   **Negative Charge (q < 0):** The electric field points radially **towards** the negative charge.
  *   If q is negative, then the force on q' is towards q, so the field is towards q.
## Visualization: Electric Field Lines
  
  Electric field lines are a visual tool to represent the electric field.
  
  **Properties of Electric Field Lines:**
  
  1. **Tangent:** The tangent to a field line at any point indicates the direction of the electric field at that point.
  2. **Density:** The density of field lines (how close together they are) is proportional to the magnitude of the electric field. Stronger fields have denser lines.
  3. **No Crossing:** Electric field lines never cross each other. If they did, it would imply that the electric field at that point has two different directions, which is not possible.
  4. **Source and Sink:** Field lines originate from positive charges (sources) and terminate on negative charges (sinks).
## Uniform Electric Field
  
  A **uniform electric field** is a region where the electric field has the same magnitude and direction at every point.
  
  **Parallel Plate Capacitor:**
  
  A common way to create a uniform electric field is using a parallel plate capacitor. This consists of two large, parallel conducting plates separated by a small distance (compared to their size).
  
  *   **Charge Distribution:** When charged, one plate carries a positive charge (+Q), and the other carries an equal and opposite negative charge (-Q).
  *   **Field:** The electric field between the plates is approximately uniform and directed from the positive plate to the negative plate. The horizontal components of the field lines cancel out, and only the vertical components remain.
  *   **Surface Charge Density (σ):**  Defined as the charge per unit area on each plate: σ = Q/A, where A is the area of one plate.
  *   **Electric Field Strength:** The magnitude of the uniform electric field between the plates is given by:
  
  $E = \frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0} = \frac{Q}{\epsilon_0 A}$
  
  Where:
  
  *   $E$: Electric field strength
  *   $\sigma$: Surface charge density
  *   $Q$: Charge on one plate
  *   $A$: Area of one plate
  *   $\epsilon_0$: Permittivity of free space (a fundamental constant, approximately 8.85 x 10<sup>-12</sup> C<sup>2</sup>/N•m<sup>2</sup>)
## Conductors in Electric Fields: Electrostatic Equilibrium
  
  **Electrostatic Equilibrium:** A conductor is in electrostatic equilibrium when there is no net flow of charge within the conductor.
  
  **Properties of Conductors in Electrostatic Equilibrium:**
  
  1. **Zero Field Inside:** The electric field inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium is zero. If there were a field, free charges within the conductor would move, creating a current, which violates the condition of equilibrium.
  2. **Charge on Surface:** Any net charge on a conductor resides entirely on its surface. This is because charges of the same sign repel and want to be as far away from each other as possible, so they are located on the surface of the conductor.
  3. **Field Perpendicular to Surface:** The electric field just outside the surface of a conductor is perpendicular (normal) to the surface. If there were a tangential component, it would cause charges to move along the surface, again violating equilibrium.
  
  **Field lines cannot cross**
  **Field lines start at positive charge and end at negative charge**
## Question
  
  What is the electric field inside a charged conductor?
  A) It points away from the center
  B) It is zero
  C) It points towards the center
  D) Cannot be determined from the given information
  
  **Solution:**
  The correct answer is **B) It is zero**
  This is because any net charge on a conductor resides entirely on its surface, as explained above.
