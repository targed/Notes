# Lecture 2: Coulomb's Law - Two-Dimensional Problems
## Review: Coulomb's Law
  
  Recall from Lecture 1 that Coulomb's Law describes the force between two point charges:
  
  $F = k \frac{|q_1||q_2|}{r^2}$
  
  Where:
  
  *   $F$ is the magnitude of the electrostatic force.
  *   $k$ is Coulomb's constant (approximately 9 x 10<sup>9</sup> N•m<sup>2</sup>/C<sup>2</sup>).
  *   $q_1$ and $q_2$ are the magnitudes of the charges.
  *   $r$ is the distance between the charges.
## Example: Adding Electrical Forces in Two Dimensions
  
  When dealing with charges that are not aligned in a straight line, we need to consider the vector nature of the forces and use vector addition to find the net force.
  
  **Procedure for Solving Two-Dimensional Problems:**
  
  1. **Diagram:** Draw a clear diagram showing the charges and their positions. Draw the force vectors acting on the charge of interest, indicating both magnitude and direction.
  2. **Magnitudes:** Use Coulomb's Law to calculate the magnitude of each individual force vector.
  3. **Components:** Resolve each force vector into its x and y components. You may need to use trigonometry (sine, cosine, tangent) based on the geometry of the problem.
  4. **Summation:**
    *   Add all the x-components of the individual forces to get the x-component of the net force.
    *   Add all the y-components of the individual forces to get the y-component of the net force.
  5. **Check:** Compare the direction and approximate magnitude of your resultant net force vector with your initial diagram. This helps to catch any errors in calculation or component determination.

### Problem 3: Equilateral Triangle
  
  **Question:** Three identical charges are located at the corners of an equilateral triangle. If the length of the sides of the triangle is doubled, what happens to the resulting force on each of the charges?
  
  A) Halved
  B) Decreases by a factor of 4
  C) Remains the same
  D) Doubles
  
  **Solution:**
  
  The correct answer is **B) Decreases by a factor of 4**.
  
  **Explanation:**
  
  *   Coulomb's Law states that the force is inversely proportional to the square of the distance between the charges ($F \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$).
  *   If the side length of the equilateral triangle is doubled (r becomes 2r), the new force (F') will be:
  
  $F' = k \frac{|q_1||q_2|}{(2r)^2} = k \frac{|q_1||q_2|}{4r^2} = \frac{1}{4} \left( k \frac{|q_1||q_2|}{r^2} \right) = \frac{1}{4}F$
  
  *   Therefore, the new force is 1/4 of the original force, meaning it decreases by a factor of 4.
