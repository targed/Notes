# Lecture 11: Test 1 Review
  
  This lecture summarizes the key concepts and equations covered in preparation for Test 1.
## 1. Charge and Coulomb's Law
  
  *   **Charge:**
    *   Two types: positive and negative.
    *   **Like charges repel**, **unlike charges attract**.
    *   Charge is conserved (cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred).
    *   Charge is quantized (exists in discrete units of the elementary charge, *e* = 1.602 x 10<sup>-19</sup> C).
  
  *   **Coulomb's Law:** Describes the force between two point charges:
  
    $F = k \frac{|q_1||q_2|}{r^2}$
  
    Where:
  
    *   $F$: Magnitude of the electrostatic force
    *   $k$: Coulomb's constant (9 x 10<sup>9</sup> N•m<sup>2</sup>/C<sup>2</sup>)
    *   $q_1$, $q_2$: Magnitudes of the charges
    *   $r$: Distance between the charges
  
  *   **Comparison to Gravity:**  Similar inverse-square law relationship, but electrostatic forces can be attractive or repulsive, while gravity is always attractive.
## 2. Electric Field
  
  *   **Concept:** A charge creates an electric field in the space around it.  Other charges experience a force due to this field.
  *   **Definition:** The electric field (**E**) at a point is the force per unit charge that a positive test charge would experience at that point:
  
    $\vec{E} = \frac{\vec{F}}{q}$  (where q is a small, positive test charge)
  
  *   **Units:** N/C
  
  *   **Electric Field of a Point Charge:**
  
    $E = k \frac{|q|}{r^2}$ (magnitude)
  
    *   **Direction:**
        *   Radially *outward* from a positive charge (q > 0).
        *   Radially *inward* toward a negative charge (q < 0).
  
  *   **Uniform Electric Field:**
    *   Created by parallel plate capacitors.
    *   Constant magnitude and direction between the plates.
    *   $E = \frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0} = \frac{Q}{\epsilon_0 A}$
       * $\sigma$: Surface charge density
       * Q: Total charge on a plate.
       * A: Area of a plate.
  
  *   **Electric Field Lines:**
    *   Visual representation of the electric field.
    *   Tangent to a field line gives the direction of **E**.
    *   Density of lines indicates field strength (closer lines = stronger field).
    *   Lines originate on positive charges and terminate on negative charges.
    *   Field lines never cross.
## 3. Conductors in Electrostatic Equilibrium
  
  *   **Key Properties:**
    *   **Zero Electric Field Inside:**  E = 0 inside the conductor.
    *   **Charge on Surface:** Any net charge resides on the surface.
    *   **Perpendicular Field Outside:** The electric field just outside the surface is perpendicular to the surface.
    *   **Equipotential Surface:** The surface of the conductor is an equipotential surface (constant potential).
## 4. Forces and Torques in Electric Fields
  
  *   **Force on a Charge:**
  
    $\vec{F} = q\vec{E}$
  
    *   If *q* is positive, **F** is in the same direction as **E**.
    *   If *q* is negative, **F** is in the opposite direction to **E**.
  
  *   **Electric Dipole in a Uniform Field:**
    *   **Dipole Moment:**  $\vec{p} = q\vec{L}$ (where L is the vector from -q to +q)
    *   **Net Force:** Zero.
    *   **Torque:** $\vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$ or $\tau = pE\sin\theta$, tending to align the dipole with the field.
## 5. Electric Potential Energy (U) and Electric Potential (V)
  
  *   **Electric Potential Energy:**
    *   Energy associated with the position of a charge in an electric field.
    *   Change in potential energy (ΔU) is the negative of the work done by the electric field:  ΔU = -W.
    * In a uniform field:
       $U_f - U_i = -qEd$ (if displacement d is along the field)
  
  *   **Electric Potential:**
    *   Potential energy per unit charge:  $V = \frac{U}{q}$
    *   Units: Volts (V) = Joules/Coulomb (J/C)
    *   Scalar quantity.
    *   **Potential Difference:** $\Delta V = V_b - V_a = \frac{\Delta U}{q} = -\frac{W_{a \rightarrow b}}{q}$
  *   **Electric Potential of a Point Charge:**
  
    $V = k \frac{q}{r}$ (assuming V = 0 at infinity)
  
  *   **Potential due to Multiple Charges:** Scalar sum of potentials due to individual charges:
  
    $V_{total} = k \sum_i \frac{q_i}{r_i}$
## 6. Energy Conservation
  
  *   **Total Energy:** $E = K + U$ (Kinetic energy + Electric Potential Energy)
  *   **Conservation:**  $K_a + U_a = K_b + U_b$  (if only electric forces act)
    $\frac{1}{2}mv_a^2 + qV_a = \frac{1}{2}mv_b^2 + qV_b$
## 7. Electron Volt (eV)
  
  *   **Definition:** Energy gained by an electron moving through a potential difference of 1 Volt.
  *   **Conversion:** 1 eV = 1.602 x 10<sup>-19</sup> J
## 8. Equipotential Surfaces
  
  *   **Definition:** Surfaces on which the electric potential (V) is constant.
  *   **Relationship to Electric Field:**
    *   Electric field lines are always *perpendicular* to equipotential surfaces.
    *   The electric field points from higher potential to lower potential.
    *   The electric field is stronger where equipotential surfaces are closer together.
## 9. Potential and Field for Important Cases
  
  | Charge Configuration       | Electric Field (E)                                                               | Electric Potential (V)                                                            |
  | :-------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
  | Point Charge               | $E = k\frac{|q|}{r^2} (radially outward/inward)                                      | $V = k\frac{q}{r}$                                                                 |
  | Uniformly Charged Sphere    | Outside:  $E = k\frac{|q|}{r^2}$ (like point charge); Inside: E = 0                   | Outside: $V = k\frac{q}{r}$; Inside: V = constant (same as surface potential)       |
  | Parallel Plate Capacitor | $E = \frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0} = \frac{Q}{\epsilon_0 A}$ (uniform between plates)      | $|\Delta V| = Ed$ (linear change between plates)                                            |
  | Infinitely Long Line of Charge| $E = \frac{2k\lambda}{r}$| $V = -2k\lambda ln(r) + C$|
## 10. Capacitors
  
  *   **Definition:** Device that stores electrical energy by accumulating charge.
  *   **Capacitance (C):**  $C = \frac{Q}{|\Delta V|}$
    *   Units: Farads (F) = Coulombs/Volt (C/V)
  *   **Parallel Plate Capacitor:** $C = \frac{\epsilon_0 A}{d}$ (without dielectric)
     $C = \kappa \frac{\epsilon_0 A}{d}$ (with dielectric)
  * **Energy Stored:** $U = \frac{1}{2} \frac{Q^2}{C} = \frac{1}{2} Q |\Delta V| = \frac{1}{2} C |\Delta V|^2$
## 11. Capacitor Networks
  
  *   **Capacitors in Parallel:**
    *   Same voltage (ΔV) across each capacitor.
    *   $C_{eq} = C_1 + C_2 + C_3 + ...$
  
  *   **Capacitors in Series:**
    *   Same charge (Q) on each capacitor.
    *   $\frac{1}{C_{eq}} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} + \frac{1}{C_3} + ...$
